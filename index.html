⚔️ 0.95 — the tightest lead this table has ever shown

Forget the bracket for a second and just look at the top two rows: Paulius on 27.39, Divisha on 26.44. That gap is 0.95 of a point — smaller than a single knockout win is worth. 💸 The fire sale that wasn't — how Divisha bought her way into contention

Two editions ago Divisha was buried mid-table on a leveraged double-Mexico-and-Spain fortress the model loved and the standings didn't. Add it up and Divisha turned a bloated, over-exposed four-team hand into a lean two-team one — Mexico ×1 (through to the Round of 16) and Argentina ×1 (unbeaten, 9 goals in 3, Messi playing the football of his life) — while banking a fat 3.44 cash cushion along the way. She cashed in the parts of the fortress that were about to get expensive to hold and bought the reigning champions for pocket change. 🗞️ UPDATE · Wednesday 8 July 2026 — the King is buried, the throne changes hands

📰 Match report · Round of 32, a Golden Boot race, and one genuine controversy

Thirteen of sixteen last-32 ties are done, the Golden Boot race has turned into a two-horse sprint, and the tournament produced its first proper refereeing storm. Here's the football — with an eye, as always, on the office ledger it keeps rewriting.

👑 Messi's record, Yamal's poise, and a fairy tale still alive

Lionel Messi is now the outright all-time World Cup goalscorer on 19, after a hat-trick against Algeria, a brace against Austria and a free-kick off the bench against Jordan — Argentina go into tonight's Round of 32 unbeaten, 9 goals scored, and heavy favourites against Cape Verde, who are officially the smallest nation by population ever to reach a men's World Cup knockout stage. It is, per one American outlet, "the best TV show of the summer" — for Divisha and Tim, who between them hold Argentina, it's simply appointment viewing. Michael, still improbably alive after his Paraguay resurrection two editions ago, now has a second miracle to root for: his double Cape Verde share needs the fairy tale to run one more night.

In the Golden Boot race, Mbappé has drawn level with Messi on 6 goals and leads on the assist tiebreak; Dembélé announced himself with a hat-trick in France's final group game. But the individual moment of the fortnight belongs to Lamine Yamal, 18, who was named Man of the Match in Spain's 3–0 dismantling of Austria without even scoring — pure ball-carrying, defenders bent around him like he was dribbling through cones. His own verdict afterwards: "Little by little, I'm feeling like myself." The man who just paid Divisha 6.75 for that Spain share, Rohit, will take that as very good value indeed.

🚩 Balogun sees red — and the pundits don't agree

The USA's 2–0 win over Bosnia & Herzegovina — which kept Paulius's lone live share alive — was overshadowed by Folarin Balogun's 64th-minute red card for a challenge that injured a Bosnian defender's ankle. The reaction split cleanly down the middle: Mauricio Pochettino called it "never a red card," Weston McKennie said it was "questionable" given "many other plays like that" went unpunished elsewhere in the tournament, and former Premier League referee Mark Clattenburg agreed the decision was "not consistent with what I've seen the rest of this tournament." ESPN's own referee analyst, by contrast, called the collision "purely accidental." Balogun's ban rules him out of the USA's Round of 16 date with Belgium — a game Pak and Margot will be watching closely on Belgium's behalf.

😤 England grind past DR Congo — Kane does it late, again

England needed a Harry Kane-inspired comeback to see off DR Congo 2–1 — nervy, backs-against-the-wall stuff that kept Neil's double England alive and cost Haydee her DR Congo share. It sets up, four days from now, the single biggest fixture on this office's calendar.

🎙️ The one everyone's talking about: Mexico v England, Sunday night at the Azteca

Confirmed, official, and circled by every desk in world football: Mexico host England in the Round of 16 at the Estadio Azteca, Mexico City — 6pm local Sunday 5 July, which is 1am Monday in the UK. Only the second meeting ever between these two nations; the first was England's 2–0 win at Wembley in 1966, the tournament they went on to win. In the office, it's a straight Neil (double England) v Divisha (single Mexico, plus Giannis's other share) derby, with a great deal riding on ninety minutes at altitude.

Thomas Tuchel isn't hiding from the elephant in the stadium: "The altitude will be, of course, a big disadvantage, because we cannot physically adapt to it in four days. It's just impossible… that is just a huge advantage that Mexico will have." He's calling the fixture itself "maybe one of the most beautiful, most exciting fixtures you can have," and leaning into the hostility: "I'm not sure if the travel will be smooth, if the sleep will be smooth, if there's noise outside the hotel… OK, bring it on." Mexico boss Javier Aguirre — in his last tournament before Rafael Márquez takes over — was blunter still about home advantage: "It is priceless. England was champion playing at home, and never again." Mexico have never lost a World Cup match at the Azteca (7W 3D in 10) and have outscored opponents 8–0 there this tournament, their best-ever start; the counter-curse is that they've lost seven straight Round-of-16 ties across seven World Cups. Bookmakers still make England moneyline favourites (+125 to Mexico's +245) despite all of it. Neil will take that. Divisha, on the back of the fortnight she's had, will fancy her chances of the upset anyway.

📰 Match report · The Round of 16 — a five-time champion dumped out, a King buried, and Ronaldo in tears

Sixteen became eight in the space of a long weekend, and it was carnage. A five-time world champion went home, the tournament's grand old man played his last-ever World Cup match and wept on the shoulder of an 18-year-old, and the office ledger took its heaviest single-round battering yet. Here is the football — with, as ever, one eye on the shares it detonated.

🇳🇴 Brazil 1–2 Norway — Haaland sends the favourites home

The result of the round, maybe of the tournament. Erling Haaland scored twice to knock five-time winners Brazil out, a stoppage-time Neymar penalty nothing more than a footnote, and carried Norway into their first-ever World Cup quarter-final. Haaland had told anyone who'd listen that this squad carried no fear — "Norway doesn't fear any team," he'd said before a ball was kicked, and on his own goalscoring he is gloriously blunt: "I'm just really good at scoring goals. I don't know what I'm doing, but that's just how it is." In the office it detonated in slow motion: CJ's Brazil was gone, while the two men holding Norway — Pak, very much alive, and Kelvin, who'd bought his share that very morning — watched a genuine dark horse gallop into the last eight.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 Mexico 2–3 England — the Azteca derby delivers, and how

The one the whole office had circled, and it did not disappoint: a 3–2 England win at the Estadio Azteca that Harry Kane summed up in four words afterwards — "It was a crazy game." "We had to find something… we found a way," the captain went on. "Unbelievable support from the fans. I'm speechless." Thomas Tuchel, who'd spent the build-up warning about the altitude, was almost lost for words himself: "When the going gets tough, they never give up, they never lose belief… It was a crazy, crazy game. We left everything out there. Now it's full steam ahead." For the office it was a straight Neil (double England) v Divisha (Mexico) derby — except Divisha had sold her Mexico that morning, so the only real skin in the game was Neil's, and his England pulled through to keep his slender survival hopes flickering.

🇪🇸 Portugal 0–1 Spain — Ronaldo's last dance, Yamal's arrival

A clash of eras settled by Mikel Merino's late strike: Spain 1, Portugal 0, and the end of the international road for Cristiano Ronaldo, who left the field in tears in what was confirmed as his final World Cup appearance — consoled, in the image that ran around the planet, by 18-year-old Lamine Yamal, the boy who has inherited the tournament from him. Kwan's Portugal was his last live share; it died on the grandest stage there is. Spain — held, agonisingly, by the eliminated Rohit — march on as many people's favourites.

🇧🇪 USA 1–4 Belgium — the rout that dethroned the King

The most consequential result on the office ledger all tournament. Belgium dismantled the hosts' co-tenants 4–1 in Seattle — Charles De Ketelaere with a brace and an assist, Malik Tillman's free-kick briefly levelling before the USA conceded again inside 61 seconds — and manager Rudi Garcia was so unbothered he left Kevin De Bruyne, Lukaku and Doku on the bench and still won at a canter. It ended Paulius's reign: his USA ×2 were his only live shares, and the King went out with them. Belgium's reward is Pak and Margot, who both still hold a share of a team now into the quarters — though only Pak is alive enough to enjoy it.

🇦🇷 Argentina 3–2 Egypt — Messi drags the champions off the floor

Two-nil down and staring at the exit, Argentina scored three unanswered from the 79th minute — Lionel Messi, now the outright all-time World Cup goalscorer, crafting the first for Romero and levelling four minutes later, having earlier missed a penalty. "It was a relief for everybody. I was very upset with the missed penalty," he admitted. That goal was his eighth of the tournament, edging him ahead of Mbappé and Haaland at the top of the Golden Boot race (ESPN noting Haaland's seven alone have outscored Messi, Mbappé and Ronaldo's combined tallies — this is a two-man sprint now). Mo Salah's Egypt went home heartbroken, taking Haydee with them; Divisha and Tim, who share Argentina between them, could finally breathe.

🇨🇭 Switzerland 0–0 Colombia (4–3 pens) — the coldest exit of all

Nil-nil after 120 minutes, Colombia the better side on the expected-goals count, and then a shootout: Rubén Vargas buried the decisive penalty to send Switzerland through 4–3. For Jing, whose Switzerland ×2 is now two wins from a semi-final, it was a night to keep the faith; for Kerrill, whose Colombia was his last live hope, it was the cruellest way to be buried — a coin-flip after two goalless hours. And in the round's two quieter ties, Morocco swept Canada aside 3–0 (ending Echo's run and boosting Tim's live stack) and France edged Paraguay 1–0 — the result that finally retired Michael's two-edition Paraguay fairy tale, and pushed Dea's double-France dream into a quarter-final she, heartbreakingly, can no longer win from.
