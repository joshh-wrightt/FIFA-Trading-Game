⚔️ 0.95 — the tightest lead this table has ever shown

Forget the bracket for a second and just look at the top two rows: Paulius on 27.39, Divisha on 26.44. That gap is 0.95 of a point — smaller than a single knockout win is worth. Two editions ago Paulius was coasting with a 1.54-point cushion and a hand of corpses; now he's clinging on with his fingertips, and the woman closing him down wasn't even in the top half of the table the last time we wrote one of these.

Paulius's book, for the record, is not the walking-dead portfolio it was — his USA ×2 survived a man-advantage scare against Bosnia and are through to the Round of 16, so the King actually has a pulse this time. But a two-share pulse is all he's got left; everything else in his hand (Czechia, Uzbekistan, Jordan, all ×2) is in the ground. Divisha, meanwhile, has spent the fortnight doing something much more interesting than sitting still.

💸 The fire sale that wasn't — how Divisha bought her way into contention

Two editions ago Divisha was buried mid-table on a leveraged double-Mexico-and-Spain fortress the model loved and the standings didn't. Since then she has run possibly the shrewdest sequence of trades in the office: she sold a Mexico to Giannis for a France and 2.00 cash, flipped Spain to Rohit for a full 6.75, sold her spare Cape Verde to Michael for 0.58, and then — in the single best piece of business anyone has done all tournament — traded that same France straight to Dea for Argentina, for a token 0.10.

Add it up and Divisha turned a bloated, over-exposed four-team hand into a lean two-team one — Mexico ×1 (through to the Round of 16) and Argentina ×1 (unbeaten, 9 goals in 3, Messi playing the football of his life) — while banking a fat 3.44 cash cushion along the way. She cashed in the parts of the fortress that were about to get expensive to hold and bought the reigning champions for pocket change. It is the single reason she is 0.95 behind the leader instead of sixteen places back.

📰 Match report · Round of 32, a Golden Boot race, and one genuine controversy

Thirteen of sixteen last-32 ties are done, the Golden Boot race has turned into a two-horse sprint, and the tournament produced its first proper refereeing storm. Here's the football — with an eye, as always, on the office ledger it keeps rewriting.

👑 Messi's record, Yamal's poise, and a fairy tale still alive

Lionel Messi is now the outright all-time World Cup goalscorer on 19, after a hat-trick against Algeria, a brace against Austria and a free-kick off the bench against Jordan — Argentina go into tonight's Round of 32 unbeaten, 9 goals scored, and heavy favourites against Cape Verde, who are officially the smallest nation by population ever to reach a men's World Cup knockout stage. It is, per one American outlet, "the best TV show of the summer" — for Divisha and Tim, who between them hold Argentina, it's simply appointment viewing. Michael, still improbably alive after his Paraguay resurrection two editions ago, now has a second miracle to root for: his double Cape Verde share needs the fairy tale to run one more night.

In the Golden Boot race, Mbappé has drawn level with Messi on 6 goals and leads on the assist tiebreak; Dembélé announced himself with a hat-trick in France's final group game. But the individual moment of the fortnight belongs to Lamine Yamal, 18, who was named Man of the Match in Spain's 3–0 dismantling of Austria without even scoring — pure ball-carrying, defenders bent around him like he was dribbling through cones. His own verdict afterwards: "Little by little, I'm feeling like myself." The man who just paid Divisha 6.75 for that Spain share, Rohit, will take that as very good value indeed.

🚩 Balogun sees red — and the pundits don't agree

The USA's 2–0 win over Bosnia & Herzegovina — which kept Paulius's lone live share alive — was overshadowed by Folarin Balogun's 64th-minute red card for a challenge that injured a Bosnian defender's ankle. The reaction split cleanly down the middle: Mauricio Pochettino called it "never a red card," Weston McKennie said it was "questionable" given "many other plays like that" went unpunished elsewhere in the tournament, and former Premier League referee Mark Clattenburg agreed the decision was "not consistent with what I've seen the rest of this tournament." ESPN's own referee analyst, by contrast, called the collision "purely accidental." Balogun's ban rules him out of the USA's Round of 16 date with Belgium — a game Pak and Margot will be watching closely on Belgium's behalf.

😤 England grind past DR Congo — Kane does it late, again

England needed a Harry Kane-inspired comeback to see off DR Congo 2–1 — nervy, backs-against-the-wall stuff that kept Neil's double England alive and cost Haydee her DR Congo share. It sets up, four days from now, the single biggest fixture on this office's calendar.

🎙️ The one everyone's talking about: Mexico v England, Sunday night at the Azteca

Confirmed, official, and circled by every desk in world football: Mexico host England in the Round of 16 at the Estadio Azteca, Mexico City — 6pm local Sunday 5 July, which is 1am Monday in the UK. Only the second meeting ever between these two nations; the first was England's 2–0 win at Wembley in 1966, the tournament they went on to win. In the office, it's a straight Neil (double England) v Divisha (single Mexico, plus Giannis's other share) derby, with a great deal riding on ninety minutes at altitude.

Thomas Tuchel isn't hiding from the elephant in the stadium: "The altitude will be, of course, a big disadvantage, because we cannot physically adapt to it in four days. It's just impossible… that is just a huge advantage that Mexico will have." He's calling the fixture itself "maybe one of the most beautiful, most exciting fixtures you can have," and leaning into the hostility: "I'm not sure if the travel will be smooth, if the sleep will be smooth, if there's noise outside the hotel… OK, bring it on." Mexico boss Javier Aguirre — in his last tournament before Rafael Márquez takes over — was blunter still about home advantage: "It is priceless. England was champion playing at home, and never again." Mexico have never lost a World Cup match at the Azteca (7W 3D in 10) and have outscored opponents 8–0 there this tournament, their best-ever start; the counter-curse is that they've lost seven straight Round-of-16 ties across seven World Cups. Bookmakers still make England moneyline favourites (+125 to Mexico's +245) despite all of it. Neil will take that. Divisha, on the back of the fortnight she's had, will fancy her chances of the upset anyway.
